Some background

What happened to Sigiriya Craft Village?

The original Sigiriya Craft Village was established to connect Sri Lankan crafts and tourism. Over time, activity at the location declined, and many travellers now encounter older online listings that may not reflect the current visitor experience.

If you are looking for somewhere in Sigiriya today to discover Sri Lankan crafts, see locally made products, or join a hands-on workshop, the Impact Center by ZeroPlastic offers a current alternative during your time in Sigiriya.

Meet the People Behind the Products

The Impact Center represents products made by 249 Sri Lankan artisans. Most of the products come directly from artisan communities in villages across the country, while selected craft experiences and demonstrations are available at the center itself, depending on the day.

The center also works with a nearby centre for the local blind community. Floor mats and incense sticks made by blind artisans are among the plastic-free pieces on the shelves.

A different kind of landmark

Meet the Elephant Made From Sigiriya's Plastic Waste

One of the most unusual things to see at the Impact Center is a life-size elephant created using plastic waste collected around Sigiriya.

Bottle crates, drum lids, toy parts and machine casings, all lifted out of Sigiriya and riveted into one animal. The sculpture helps visitors see discarded plastic differently, and creates a memorable way to talk about recycling and responsible tourism.

Its belly is open on purpose. Visitors can place suitable empty plastic bottles into the elephant for recycling.

Questions About Sigiriya Craft Village

Everything travellers ask us when they find this page while searching for Sigiriya Craft Village or crafts near Sigiriya Rock.

Is Sigiriya Craft Village open?

Older online listings still refer to the historic Sigiriya Craft Village. Travellers looking for a current craft attraction in Sigiriya can visit the Impact Center by ZeroPlastic for artisan products, sustainability experiences and hands-on workshops.

Where can I see Sri Lankan crafts in Sigiriya?

The Impact Center by ZeroPlastic in Sigiriya displays products made by Sri Lankan artisans, alongside craft demonstrations and hands-on workshops, free to walk in and see.

Where can I buy handmade souvenirs in Sigiriya?

The Impact Center's shelves hold more than 1,000 plastic-free products made by local artisans, from coconut shell and palm to reclaimed timber. Every piece is for sale, and browsing the shelves is free.

Are there craft workshops in Sigiriya?

Yes. Selected hands-on craft experiences, including coconut shell carving and traditional mask painting, are available at the Impact Center. Book a workshop in advance to confirm availability.

Is the Impact Center free to visit?

Yes. General entry is free. Hands-on workshops and the Tree of Life experience are optional, paid experiences.

Is the Impact Center suitable for children?

The Impact Center is a family-friendly stop in Sigiriya. Children can see crafts being made, learn how materials are reused, and join selected workshops with their families.

Can I book a craft experience in advance?

Yes. Hands-on experiences and the Tree of Life experience can be booked in advance at book.zeroplastic.lk.

Where is the Impact Center in Sigiriya?

The Impact Center by ZeroPlastic is in Sigiriya, Central Province, Sri Lanka. Directions are available on the map above.

How long should I spend at the Impact Center?

A general visit can be as short or as long as you like. Hands-on workshop time depends on the experience you choose.

Can I meet local artisans?

The products at the Impact Center are made by 249 Sri Lankan artisans from villages across the country. Selected artisans and team members demonstrate crafts at the center depending on the day and schedule.
