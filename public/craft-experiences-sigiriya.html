What happens during a workshop

You Sit at the Bench, Not Behind a Rope

These are working benches, not a demonstration stage. You are shown the technique, then you do it.

Arrive and look around

Walk in free of charge. See the elephant built from salvaged plastic and the shelves of finished work before anything starts.

Meet the artisan

The person leading your session shows you the material, the tools and what the finished piece should look like.

Make it yourself

You work the material at the bench with guidance beside you. Hands get dirty. That is the point.

Take it home

In the coconut shell and mask painting sessions, the piece you made goes with you. Browsing the shop afterwards stays free, as always.

Who you'll be working with

Local Artisans, Not Performers

The Impact Center has 249 artisan families represented on its shelves. Most products come directly from artisan communities in villages across the country, while selected craft experiences and demonstrations run at the centre itself, depending on the day.

The centre also works with a nearby centre for the local blind community. Floor mats and incense sticks made by blind artisans are among the plastic-free pieces on the shelves.

Why the workshops exist

Your Session Keeps a Craft in Work

The Impact Center is run by ZeroPlastic Movement, a Sri Lankan nonprofit. Workshop fees support the artisans who lead them and the plastic-free craft work the centre exists to sustain — supporting 249 artisan families through our work, more than 1,000 plastic-free products, and an elephant built from plastic salvaged around Sigiriya.

Before you come

Questions We Get Asked

How much does a craft workshop cost?

Pricing depends on which experience you choose and is confirmed when you enquire. Send the form above and the team will reply with availability and pricing for the workshop and date you asked about.

Do I have to book in advance?

Booking ahead is recommended, since availability depends on the artisan and the day. The Tree of Life experience requires advance booking. Walking in to look around needs no booking at all.

Is it really free to visit?

Yes. Entry, observation and browsing the shelves are free, every day. Only the hands-on workshops and the Tree of Life experience are paid.

Can children take part?

The Impact Center is a family-friendly stop in Sigiriya. Children can see crafts being made, learn how materials are reused, and join selected workshops with their families. Mention their ages in your enquiry and the team will suggest the best fit.

How long does a workshop take?

It depends on the experience and how much you want to finish on the day. Ask in your enquiry and the team will give you a realistic time for the session you have in mind.

Do I take home what I make?

In the coconut shell craft session you take home what you shape, and in traditional mask painting you take home the mask you painted. For the other experiences, ask the team when you enquire.

Where exactly are you?

In Sigiriya, Central Province, roughly 5 km before Sigiriya Lion Rock on the main approach road. Open every day from 7:30 to 18:30. Use the Google Maps link above for turn-by-turn directions.
