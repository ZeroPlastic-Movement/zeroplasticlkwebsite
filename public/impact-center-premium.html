Things to Do in Sigiriya

Made from what Sigiriya once threw away.

The ZeroPlastic Impact Center is a free place to visit near Sigiriya Rock — one of the more unusual things to do in Sigiriya. Meet local Sri Lankan artisans, see what salvaged plastic becomes, and browse more than 1,000 plastic-free products. Open daily, no ticket needed.

What to see

Four free things to do here.

Walk in off the road. Stay ten minutes or stay the whole afternoon. Tell us you're coming and someone will be free to sit with you. Want to go further? Paid hands-on experiences and the complete Tree of Life story are just below.

Free

Outdoors · about 10 min · walk up any time

Meet the elephant

Bottle crates, drum lids, toy parts, machine casings — all of it lifted out of Sigiriya and riveted into one animal. Its belly is open on purpose. Drop in a piece of plastic you've picked up on your travels and it becomes part of the sculpture.

Free

At the bench · a short demonstration · ask any time

See a bottle lid transformed

Ask the team to demonstrate how a discarded bottle lid can be transformed into a small ornament or fridge magnet — sorted, shredded and pressed right at the bench. It's a demonstration you watch from start to finish, not a hands-on workshop.

Free

In the studio · stay as long as you like · let us know first

Observe artisans at work

ZeroPlastic's in-house craftspeople work here most days — coconut shell carved into bowls and jewellery, traditional mask painting, and more. When craftmaking is under way, pull up a chair and watch for as long as you like, and ask anything. Observing is free. Trying it yourself is a paid, availability-permitting experience — see hands-on experiences below.

Free to browse

Along the shelves · any time · pick things up

Discover plastic-free products

More than 1,000 plastic-free products, souvenirs, ornaments, gifts and practical plastic alternatives, made by 249 artisans in villages across Sri Lanka — coconut shell, palm, clay, reed, reclaimed timber. Turn them over, ask how each one is made and who made it — that part costs nothing. Everything on the shelves is for sale, and every purchase goes back to the artisan behind it.

Planning your trip

How it fits a day in Sigiriya.

Most people come to Sigiriya for the Rock. This is somewhere to put the rest of the day — a short, free stop that doesn't need a ticket, a booking or a whole morning. If you are working through a list of places to visit in Sigiriya and want something other than another viewpoint, Impact Center Sigiriya is an easy addition.

Coming to climb Sigiriya Rock
We're about 5 km before the Rock on the main road. Thirty minutes here before or after the climb, while it's too hot to be on the steps.
Staying in Dambulla or Habarana
Both are a short drive away. The centre sits on the route most people already take between Dambulla, Habarana and Sigiriya, so it slots into a Cultural Triangle day without a detour.
Travelling with children
Children are welcome and there's nothing to keep quiet for. As family activities in Sigiriya go, it is an unfussy one: they can get close to the elephant sculpture, watch carving happen and handle finished pieces. Entry is free, so a short visit costs nothing if they lose interest.
Looking for something calmer as a couple
It's an unhurried place to walk around, talk to the artisans and see how the work is made. No queue, no timed entry.
Interested in sustainable travel
The centre exists because plastic waste cost people their traditional livelihoods. Everything on the shelves is a plastic-free alternative made by local artisans. Walking around and understanding that is free; buying something or booking a workshop is how it's funded.
Short on time
Twenty minutes is enough to see the elephant, walk the shelves and meet whoever is working that day.

If it's specifically the hands-on making you're after — coconut shell carving, mask painting, recycled plastic craft — those are paid workshops with their own page: craft workshops in Sigiriya.

Why this place exists

Plastic didn't only foul Sri Lanka. It put people out of work.

For generations these villages made what they needed from coconut shell, palm, clay and reed. Cheap plastic undercut all of it, and the families who had made those things for a living lost their income and, slowly, the skill itself. What got thrown away went into the scrub and the waterways, and into the animals that people travel here to see. The Impact Center pushes back on both halves of that at once.

  • Shown by the people who actually do itNot a scripted demo for tourists. The artisans at the bench are the artisans who make the work, on an ordinary working day.
  • 249 artisans, more than 1,000 piecesMade in villages far from the tourist road, from natural and reused material. Handle any of it and ask who made it — buying it is what puts income back in an artisan's hands.
  • Not a scrap of plastic or polytheneNot in the studio, not on the shelves, not at the tea table. Herbal tea comes in a coconut shell.
  • A partnership with the local blind communityZeroPlastic also works with a nearby centre for the local blind community. Floor mats and incense sticks made by blind artisans are among the plastic-free pieces on the shelves.
  • Turning up is what keeps it goingThe centre is run by ZeroPlastic Movement, a Sri Lankan nonprofit and the largest volunteer environmental movement in the country. Visitors are the reason it can keep doing the work.
Before you leave

Scan the code. Add your voice.

There's a QR code by the door. Scan it and you make a pledge: to cut the single-use plastic you carry, and to say something when you see it thrown away. It takes a minute and it costs nothing.

It is also the part of the visit that goes home with you. Sri Lanka's beaches, its scrub and its elephants are up against a problem that no clean-up on its own will fix. What changes it is enough people refusing the thing in the first place, and being loud about it.

Before you come

Questions we get asked.

What is the ZeroPlastic Impact Center in Sigiriya?

It's a working craft and environmental education centre run by ZeroPlastic Movement, a Sri Lankan nonprofit, on the main road in Sigiriya. You can see a life-size elephant built from salvaged plastic, watch local artisans working, and browse more than 1,000 plastic-free products made by 249 artisan families.

Is it free to visit?

Yes. Entry and looking around are free, every day. There's no ticket and no booking. Products are for sale and hands-on craft workshops are paid, but neither is required — plenty of people walk in, look around and leave.

Do I need to book?

Not for a normal visit — just turn up during opening hours. Booking ahead only applies to paid experiences: hands-on craft workshops, and the Tree of Life experience, which requires advance booking.

Where exactly is it?

Impact Center Sigiriya is on the main Sigiriya road, about 5 km before Sigiriya Rock, in Sigiriya, Central Province. It's an easy stop from Dambulla or Habarana.

What are the opening hours?

Open every day, 7:30 to 18:30.

Is it suitable for children?

Children are welcome. They can get close to the sculpture, watch artisans carve and handle finished work. Since entry is free, a short visit costs nothing if they've had enough.

How long does a visit take?

Twenty to thirty minutes is typical for a free walk-in visit. A paid hands-on workshop takes longer and is arranged separately.

Is this the same as the craft workshops?

No. The free visit is looking around. The hands-on craft workshops are a paid, separate activity where you make something yourself — see craft workshops in Sigiriya for those.
